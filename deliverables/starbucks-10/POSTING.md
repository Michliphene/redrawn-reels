What's in your cup is about a third. Running the coffeehouse costs almost half.

## Ready replies
- **"A latte costs more than $10"** → "The $10 is a unit, not a menu price: it's how every $10 of Starbucks' revenue is split, from the 10-K."
- **"Why 97¢ then 67¢?"** → "97¢ is operating profit, before interest and tax. Net earnings were $2.53B, about 67¢ per $10, and that already includes a $536M one-off gain on the China divestiture."
- **"That's one drink?"** → "No, it's the average across the whole company in fiscal 2026, including licensed stores and grocery products. Starbucks doesn't publish the split of a single cup."
- **"Why closing stores?"** → "The 10-K books $688.6M of restructuring and impairments in fiscal 2026, tied to coffeehouse and office closures. We don't speculate on why."

- **"Licensed?"** → "Licensed coffeehouses are run by partner companies, not by Starbucks itself (China moved to licensed in 2026). 28,210 of 41,607 at the end of fiscal 2026, per the 10-K."
