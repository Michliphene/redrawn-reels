## Description (YouTube)
```
At school, copying is cheating. Out here, the rules flip: copying to learn is how you practise.

A real one: Benjamin Franklin leaves school at ten. As a young apprentice, he finds a volume of The Spectator, a London paper, and wants to write like that. So he makes short notes on each sentence, puts the book away for a few days, rewrites the essays from his notes alone, then compares his copy with the original, finds his faults and fixes them. Later, he writes and prints Poor Richard's Almanack.

The line: copy someone's work and sign it? Still cheating. Copy to learn? That's practice.
"Ten copies teach you more than one blank page" is an idea, not a study.
```
