Google's head of people said GPAs were "worthless" for hiring. Here's what he said, what the reel draws as an illustration, and why it is only one company's story.

In a June 2013 New York Times interview, Laszlo Bock, then senior vice president of people operations at Google, said GPAs and test scores were worthless as a criteria for hiring (with a slight link only for brand-new college grads), that performance after two or three years was "completely unrelated" to school performance, and that brainteasers were "a complete waste of time". The reel's chart (grades vs. how well people do the job) and the team of seven are illustrations, not Google's data. Careful: one company, one interview, in 2013, and many employers still use GPA.
