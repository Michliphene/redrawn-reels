## Description (YouTube)
```
Einstein didn't fail math. He failed an exam, and only some parts of it.

In 1935, a rabbi in Princeton showed Einstein a newspaper clipping: "Greatest living mathematician failed in mathematics." He laughed: "I never failed in mathematics. Before I was fifteen I had mastered differential and integral calculus."

What did happen: in 1895, at sixteen, about two years younger than the other applicants, he sat the entrance exam of the Zurich Polytechnic (today's ETH). Math and physics: excellent. Languages and other subjects: not enough. Overall: not admitted. The director advised him to finish secondary school first, so he spent a year at the cantonal school in Aarau. His certificate of 1896: algebra 6, geometry 6, physics 6, history 6, French 3, on a scale where 6 is the best grade. That autumn he entered the Polytechnic.

Where the myth comes from is debated. One theory: during his year in Aarau the school reversed its grading scale (1 had been the best grade), so his sixes could be misread as fails.

A fail is a total. Next time, read the parts.

Sources
- Walter Isaacson, "Making the Grade", TIME, 5 April 2007 (and Einstein: His Life and Universe, 2007).
```
