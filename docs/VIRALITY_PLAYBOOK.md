# REDRAWN virality playbook — the codes the virality agent scores against

Evidence levels: **[OFFICIAL]** stated by the platform · **[RESEARCH]** peer-reviewed / academic ·
**[PRACTITIONER]** consensus of creators/agencies (pattern, not proof). Nothing here guarantees virality;
the agent scores *odds*, never promises.

## 1. What the platforms actually rank on
| Platform | Signals | Level |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok FYP | user interactions (watching to the end, **rewatching**, likes, shares, comments, "not interested"), video information (captions, sounds, hashtags), device/account settings. Follower count is not a direct input; each video is tested on a small cohort then expanded. | [OFFICIAL] TikTok newsroom "How TikTok recommends videos #ForYou" (2020) |
| Instagram Reels | top 3 signals: **watch time**, **likes per reach**, **sends per reach** (DM shares; best predictor of reach to non-followers). Hashtags don't improve reach. Originality enforced (reposts/watermarks cut from cold distribution). | [OFFICIAL] Adam Mosseri, Jan 2025 (+2024 Q&A) |
| YouTube Shorts | **"Viewed vs. swiped away"** (hook metric), engaged views, retention. | [OFFICIAL] YouTube Help Center |
| Popularity research | creator popularity is the strongest predictor; content features matter but less (we can't change that one — so every other lever must be maxed). | [RESEARCH] Ling et al., arXiv 2111.02452 |

**So for us:** completion + rewatch (TikTok), watch time + sends (Reels), not swiping in second 1 (Shorts).

## 2. The hook (0–3 s) — the gate
1. **Three layers in the first 1–2 s:** visual (first frame must work *paused and muted*), verbal line, on-screen text (3–7 words). [PRACTITIONER]
2. **Payload in the first sentence:** a concrete number, a named stake, a contradiction or a question with a *defined shape* (curiosity gap needs a category for the answer — Loewenstein information-gap). Vague teases fail. [PRACTITIONER + theory]
3. **No throat-clearing:** no logo, no "welcome", no title-card-only frame, no slow zoom on nothing. Branding at the end. [PRACTITIONER]
4. **Promise = payoff.** Bait-and-switch wins the stop and loses the satisfaction signals. [PRACTITIONER, MrBeast memo]
5. **Pattern interrupt / surprise** attracts gaze (Itti & Baldi: gaze shifts go to surprising locations). [RESEARCH]
6. Hook archetypes that fit map-history: *big number* ("$7.2M for this"), *contrarian / myth-bust* ("Sykes-Picot didn't draw these borders"), *comparison* ("one country became fifteen"), *question* ("Why is this border a straight line?"), *in-media-res* (map already moving on frame 0).

## 3. Retention (3 s → end)
- Spine **Hook → Escalation → Payoff → (loop)**; link beats with "but / therefore", not "and then". [PRACTITIONER]
- **Something new on screen every ≤2–3 s** (camera move, label, sticker, card). On a single-map video, cue density replaces cuts. [PRACTITIONER]
- **Mid-video re-hook** around 40–60 % ("But here's the twist…") where curves sag. [PRACTITIONER]
- **Front-load:** second-strongest moment early, strongest as payoff; never bury the payoff. [PRACTITIONER]
- **Dual narrative:** voice says one thing, visuals add a complementary layer. [PRACTITIONER]
- Speech pace ~2.5–3.2 words/s for educational voice-over; captions word-by-word (sound-off viewing). [PRACTITIONER]
- Working completion benchmarks (vendor-reported, unverified): ~70 % at 15–30 s, ~50 % at 30–60 s → **shorter wins if it loses nothing**.

## 4. Rewatch & loop
- Last line completed by the first line; last frame visually ≈ first frame (we measure SSIM). [PRACTITIONER]
- Dense frames worth pausing (a map you want to re-read) add rewatches. [PRACTITIONER]

## 5. Shareability (sends) & saves
- **Send triggers:** "I didn't know that" fact, a myth busted, a comparison people argue about, identity ("for my Alaska friends"), a useful mental model. Ask: *who would this be sent to, and with what message?*
- **Save triggers:** reference value (a map you'd want later, a series to complete).
- **Comment triggers (honest):** a genuine question tied to the payoff ("Which border should we redraw next?"), a debated point flagged neutrally. Never rage-bait with false claims.

## 6. Packaging (outside the video)
- **Cover frame** readable as a thumbnail (big shape + 3–5 words). **Caption**: keywords people search (Reels/TikTok search = caption + on-screen text, not hashtags); 3–5 relevant hashtags max. [OFFICIAL Mosseri on hashtags + PRACTITIONER]
- **Series cue** ("REDRAWN #2") builds follow intent; part-2 hooks for follow-ups.
- **Trust signals** for our niche: sources in pinned comment, "approximate"/"disputed" tags — protects against "this is wrong" comment pile-ons that kill shares.

## 7. Map-history specific codes
- **Name the place within 1 s** (label/arrow/locator): non-map people must never wonder "where is this?".
- **One transformation, visible:** colour change / border move the viewer can *see*, not just hear.
- **Scale anchors:** compare to something familiar (Texas, France, "the size of Western Europe").
- **Faces & stakes:** a real person (portrait) or a human cost makes it a story, not a lesson.
- **Myth-busting** and "straight line" questions are proven formats for maps.
- **US/UK audience:** imperial units first (miles), US/UK reference points.

## 8. Anti-patterns (score penalties)
Title card without visual on frame 0 · logo/intro · vague tease · >3 s with nothing new · wall of text · tiny labels ·
captions covering the subject · fact that viewers can't verify (no source) · politically loaded framing on a disputed
topic · hook promise not paid off · ending with "follow for more" instead of a loop · music louder than voice.

## 9. DA 2 codes (all series) — what the reviewers check on top of the above
- **Frame 0 shows depth**: the opening crane shot reads as an object in a space, with the title on it. A flat
  diagram on frame 0 is an anti-pattern in DA 2.
- **Safe area**: nothing readable above y=250, below y=1440, or right of x=930 under y=860 (platform header,
  description and action buttons). A hook hidden under the interface is a lost hook.
- **One accent**: only keywords and the number that matters take the series accent. Two accents = no accent.
- **Camera as retention**: one move per beat, one cut as the mid-video re-hook, a pull back before the payoff.
  A camera move counts as "something new on screen" for the ≤ 3 s rule; a caption does not.
- **In-world information**: if a fact is shown as a floating widget where a piece of the world could show it
  (a stack, a pawn, a piece that moves), score it down.
- **Formats**: second-person ("you are…") and guess-before-the-count reels must pay off in the world, on screen.
- **Length** [PRACTITIONER, unverified against TikTok's own pages]: third-party 2026 guides agree that Creator
  Rewards only counts videos longer than one minute. The studio therefore writes every new reel for 61–70 s of voice from the script stage; never pad a short script with
  filler or slow the voice. Add a second fact, a stake or a twist, and keep the mid-video re-hook.
