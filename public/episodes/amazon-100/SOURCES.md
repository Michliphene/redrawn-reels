# RECEIPTS #007 — Where $100 at Amazon goes (the cloud pays)

Format: **A. Explainer**, one $100 of Amazon's sales cut in two (shopping vs AWS), then what each half keeps.
Primary filing: **Amazon.com, Inc., Form 10-K, fiscal 2025** (year ended 31 December 2025), filed with the SEC on
6 Feb 2026 (accession 0001018724-26-000004, `amzn-20251231.htm`). Read directly from sec.gov on 19 Nov 2026
(local session, SEC reachable). Figures in US$ millions, GAAP.

## Why this topic (backlog 007)
Everyone has bought from Amazon; almost nobody knows AWS. One filing, two numbers that flip each other
(18 % of sales, 57 % of operating profit), no figure needs a non-GAAP measure. Layout change after #006 (the cut
burrito): no slicing of an object, no grid of 100 squares. New station: 100 coins split into two piles, a parcel
pile and a server rack; the profit coins rise from each pile.

## Script (voice)
1. Amazon took in $717 billion last year. Most of the profit came from something you never see.
2. Take $100 of it.
3. $82 is shopping: the store, the sellers, the ads, Prime.
4. $18 is AWS: computing power it rents to other companies.
5. Shopping is the expensive part. Shipping alone cost $103 billion.
6. So of its $82, shopping keeps $4.79.
7. Of its $18, AWS keeps $6.36.
8. The smaller slice makes 57% of Amazon's operating profit.
9. That's $45.6 billion in 2025, from renting computers.
10. You see the boxes. The money is in the cloud.
11. So next time a box lands on your doorstep… → (loop) Amazon took in $717 billion last year.

## Simplifications and debated points (be ready for comments)
- **"Keeps" = segment operating income**, before interest, other income and tax. Not net income.
- **Segment profit depends on Amazon's own allocation**: technology and infrastructure costs "are allocated to
  segments based on usage"; shared facilities likewise. A different allocation would move the split.
- **Advertising is inside "shopping"** (North America + International). Amazon does not disclose the profit of ads
  alone: never say "ads make the money" or "the store loses money" (both segments are profitable in 2025).
- **Shipping $103B is consolidated**; the 10-K does not split it by segment. Wording: "Shopping is the expensive
  part. Shipping alone cost…" (no claim that AWS pays none).
- **"Never see"**: framing. AWS customers are businesses; many apps you use run on it, but that is not claimed.
- 2025 other operating expense ($4.6B) includes the FTC settlement (Q3 2025): not used on screen.
- Not financial advice.
