# EP 5 — Who owns Antarctica? (1908–today) · sources & fact sheet

Format: **A · explainer with two twists** (the treaty voids the "baby race"; the biggest unclaimed land on Earth).
Topic #16 of the bible ("How Antarctica got 7 claims — and none count"), angle sharpened at scripting.

## Script (as spoken)
1. Seven countries claim Antarctica. And three of them claim the same slice.
2. 1908: Britain cuts the first slice.
3. New Zealand, France, Australia and Norway take theirs.
4. Then Chile claims a slice right on top of Britain's. And Argentina claims one on top of both.
5. A 1959 treaty freezes it all. No new claims. No bigger ones.
6. But in 1978, Argentina flies a pregnant woman to its base. Her son, Emilio Palma, is the first person known to be born in Antarctica.
7. Chile's first baby follows in 1984.
8. Under the treaty, none of it counts.
9. And the US and Russia? They recognise no one's claim, and keep the right to make their own.
10. And one slice, nobody claims at all. More than twice the size of Texas. The biggest unclaimed land on Earth.
11. So who owns Antarctica? Depends who you ask. Because… → (loop) "Seven countries claim Antarctica."

Wording choices: "Then Chile… And Argentina…" orders the two claims in the script only loosely — Chile's decree is
dated 6 Nov 1940; Argentina's formal claim is dated variously 1939–1947 (see below), so the screen shows
"Argentina: dates vary, 1940s" and no year for it. No motive is stated for the 1978 birth on screen or in voice
(widely reported as a sovereignty move, see Atlas Obscura) — the viewer sees the base inside the triple overlap.

## Debated / limits (logged)
- Argentina's claim date: dates vary (1939–1947) → no year on screen.
- Australia: claim instrument 1933, accepted by law 1936.
- Norway's inland limit was undefined until 2015 → drawn to the pole like the others (status today).
- Claims are drawn on land + ice shelves only; at sea most run to 60°S (not drawn).
- Peter I Island (Norway, in the unclaimed sector) is too small for 1:50m data → not drawn, not mentioned.
- "First person born in Antarctica": Guinness / standard references; "known to be" is kept in the voice.
- Mutual recognition: the UK, NZ, France, Australia and Norway recognise each other's claims — the script never says
  "nobody recognises any claim"; it says the US and Russia recognise none.
- Flags shown are today's flags (identical or near-identical designs in the years shown).

## Map
Natural Earth 1:50m (public domain) via world-atlas 2.0.2; claim sectors cut at the published meridians;
overlaps are separate pieces (CG = UK+Chile, TRI = UK+Chile+Argentina, AG = UK+Argentina). Projection: azimuthal
