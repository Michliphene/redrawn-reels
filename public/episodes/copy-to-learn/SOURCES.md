# UNGRADED #005 — Ten copies beat one blank page

Format: D. myth-bust in the Rule Flip frame of #001 ("copying is cheating" → shown, true at school, then flipped),
plus one real, sourced case (Benjamin Franklin's *Spectator* exercise) and a short "Picture this" in three steps.
Notebook art direction, built in raea (`raea/ungraded-005-copy`), like the raea edition of #004.
Audience: teens to thirties. Tone: simple, encouraging. Message: copying to learn (copy, compare, fix, then make
your own) is practice; copying and signing someone else's work is still cheating, and the reel says so.
Idea: our own ("Copying is how every craftsperson starts" in `docs/UNGRADED_TRAME.md`, idea 2). The Rule Flip frame
is the one of #001 (itself inspired by a TikTok by @cryothum, script rewritten from scratch).

## Debated points (be ready for comments)
- **Franklin did not copy word for word.** He rebuilt each essay from his notes, then compared it with the original.
  The script says "rewrites the essays from his notes alone" and "compares his copy with the original" for that
  reason; we never show him tracing the text.
- **Copying vs. plagiarism.** The reel says on screen that copying someone's work and signing it is still cheating.
  The flip is about copying *to learn*, not about handing in someone else's work.
- **"Ten copies teach you more"** is an opinion in the frame of #001, not a finding. No study is cited for it.
- Franklin's exact age during the exercise is not given in the *Autobiography*; we say "young apprentice".
- Franklin's writing career has many causes (reading, printing, newspapers); we do not say the exercise made him
  famous, only what he did and what he wanted.

## Pinned comment
Franklin describes the exercise himself in his Autobiography: notes on each sentence of a Spectator essay, the book
put away for a few days, the essay rewritten from the notes, then compared with the original to find his faults.
He did it at night, early in the morning and on Sundays, and he only hoped to become "a tolerable English writer".
Copying to learn is practice; signing someone else's work is still cheating. What did you learn by copying?
