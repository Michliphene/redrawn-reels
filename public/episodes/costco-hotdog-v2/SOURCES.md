# RECEIPTS #001 — Why this hot dog is still $1.50 (Costco)

## Script (voice)
1. This hot dog has cost $1.50 since 1985.
2. With inflation, it should cost over four dollars. So how does Costco make money?
3. Follow $100 through the checkout.
4. About $89 goes straight to buying the products.
5. Around $9 more pays the staff and runs the warehouses.
6. What's left? Less than $2.
7. On almost $300 billion of sales, that's under $6 billion.
8. But here's the trick. You paid before you walked in.
9. The membership card. Last fiscal year, 84 million members paid $5.9 billion in fees.
10. That's more than Costco earned from everything on its shelves.
11. And every year, about nine in ten members renew.
12. So the low prices aren't the profit. They're the reason you keep the card.
13. Which helps explain why… → (loop) this hot dog has cost $1.50 since 1985.

## Simplifications and debated points (be ready for comments)
- **"Left from selling goods" ($5.8B) is our own computation**, not a line Costco reports: operating income minus
  membership fees. It assumes every operating cost is charged against merchandise and none against the fees.
  That is the standard way analysts present it, but serving members is not literally free.
- **SG&A is more than "staff and warehouses"**: it also covers utilities, card-processing fees, depreciation,
  central and regional offices. Wages and benefits are the largest part. The on-screen label is a shorthand.
- **Hot dog combo:** the $1.50 buys a hot dog *and* a soda. In 2025 a bottled-water option was added; price unchanged.
- **We do not say the hot dog loses money.** Costco has never disclosed its cost. Widely repeated, not sourced.
- **Causality is stated softly on purpose.** "Low prices are the reason you keep the card" is an interpretation
  consistent with how Costco describes its own model (low prices drive membership loyalty and fee revenue). The
  loop line says "helps explain", not "is why".
- **Inflation figure** depends on the month and index chosen, hence "over four dollars", never a precise number.
- **One-off in FY2026:** Q4 included a non-recurring benefit from tariff refunds ($0.15 per diluted share). It does
  not change any rounded figure used here. FY2025 gives the same picture (fees 5,323 vs 5,060 from selling).
- "Last fiscal year" = fiscal 2026 (ended August 2026). Re-check all figures if reposted after October 2027.
