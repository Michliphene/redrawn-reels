## Simplifications and debated points (be ready for comments)
- **Remuneration is revenue, not profit.** The $8.2B is what Amex pays; Delta still has to honour the miles with
  seats. We compare it with operating profit as a *size* comparison ("more than Delta's entire profit"), never
  "Delta makes all its profit from the card".
- **We never say flying loses money.** Delta does not disclose a profit split between flying and the card.
  Analysts who claim "without Amex Delta would barely break even" are not quoted on screen.
- **Accounting:** Delta does not book the $8.2B as one revenue line. It allocates it between travel (miles redeemed,
  booked as passenger revenue later), brand/marketing and other elements. "American Express paid $8.2 billion" is
  the cash-remuneration figure Delta itself reports, so the sentence is literal.
- **$91 / $9 are of GAAP revenue ($63.4B).** Delta's adjusted revenue ($58.3B) strips refinery sales to third
  parties; on that basis operating margin is ≈ 10 %. Either way ≈ $9–10 of $100: on-screen "about $9".
- **"Spending on Amex cards"** = Amex "billed business" (worldwide). **"Lends"** = Card Member loans. Both from
  third-party summaries of the Amex 10-K; percentages are Amex's own rounding.
- **"Last year"** = calendar/fiscal 2025. Delta's 2026 guidance (~$9B) is not shown as a fact on screen; the
  $10B is presented as a target ("aiming for").
- Not financial advice; no card recommendation; no claim about whether the card is worth it.
