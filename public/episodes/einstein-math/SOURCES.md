# UNGRADED #008 — He failed. Not math.

Format: A. myth-bust around one sourced document (Einstein's 1896 Aarau school-leaving certificate) and one sourced
episode (the 1935 Ripley's clipping), then the series' rule flip ("a fail sounds like a verdict" → "it's a list"), one
instruction (read the parts), and a loop back to the headline.
Art direction: a **report-card board** on an 1890s school wall, built in raea (`raea/ungraded-008-einstein`): one row
per subject, each grade a chunky wooden tile that rolls to its number, a 1–6 scale ruler that flips. Not the notebook of
#004/#005, not the turntable of #006, not the desk of #007.
Audience: teens to thirties, students. Tone: calm, a bit cheeky. Message: a fail is a total; read the parts.

## Debated points (be ready for comments)
- **Where the myth comes from** is not settled. The Aarau scale flip is one explanation; the failed 1895 ETH exam is the
  other (he did fail an exam, just not the math part). The reel shows both.
- **Which sections he failed in 1895** varies by source: "linguistic and historical subjects" (Rare Historical),
  "language, botany and zoology" (history.com via SBS). The reel says "languages and the rest".
- **When the Ripley's column ran** is not given by the sources; only the 1935 meeting is dated. The clipping is labelled
  "Ripley's Believe It or Not! column", undated.
- **"Calculus before fifteen"** is Einstein's own claim in his reply, not a school record.

## Pinned comment
Einstein never failed math. In 1895, at sixteen, he failed the Zurich Polytechnic entrance exam overall: top marks in
math and physics, not enough in languages and other subjects. A year later his Aarau certificate read algebra 6,
geometry 6, physics 6, on a scale where 6 is best. No scrolling back: what did he get in French? And which part of your last bad grade actually failed?
