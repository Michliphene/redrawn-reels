# UNGRADED #001 (notebook cut) — Ten failures vs. one A+

Same script, voice and facts as `fail-ten-times` (this cut reuses its audio); only the art direction differs:
ballpoint stick figures on squared paper (`composition: Doodle`). Everything drawn here, no image asset.

Format: D. myth-bust ("failing means falling behind" → shown, then flipped). An opinion piece: the only factual
claim on screen is the Dyson figure. Everything else is framed as a way of thinking, never as a statistic.
Idea inspired by a TikTok by @cryothum (script rewritten from scratch, own art direction, no footage reused).

## Script (voice)
1. Ten failures teach you more than a perfect record.
2. Most people think failing means falling behind.
3. And at school, they're right. Every mistake costs you a grade.
4. But out here, the rules flip.
5. Nobody counts your mistakes. They count what finally works.
6. Picture launching an online store.
7. Product one flops. Product two gets ignored. Product three sells a little.
8. Each miss tells you what people actually want.
9. James Dyson says he built 5,126 failed prototypes before one worked.
10. Failure only gets expensive when you stop.
11. Keep going, and every failure becomes information.
12. Ten failures? Ten lessons.
13. A perfect record? Zero.
14. So the next time you fail, remember… → (loop) ten failures teach you more than a perfect record.

## Debated points (be ready for comments)
- **How long Dyson took varies by source**: "five years" (prototype period, Wikipedia / Foundation), "15 years"
  (Inc., counting earlier work). We show no duration and no year on screen for that reason.
- **The hook is an opinion, not a finding.** We dropped the original's "the student who fails the most will make the
  most money": no source supports a causal link between failing and income (survivorship bias). We say "teach",
  not "earn".
- Failing is not free for everyone (money, time, safety nets differ). The script says failure gets expensive when
  you stop; it does not say failure costs nothing.
