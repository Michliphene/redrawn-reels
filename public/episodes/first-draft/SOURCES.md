# UNGRADED #002 — He rewrote one ending 39 times

Format: D. myth-bust on the "Rule Flip" template (`docs/UNGRADED_TRAME.md`). Notebook art direction (`Doodle`).
Factual claims: Hemingway's own count (39) and the number of endings in his papers (47). Everything else is an idea.

## Script (voice)
1. Hemingway rewrote one ending 39 times.
2. That's his count. His papers hold 47. Some are a single line.
3. Most people think a bad first draft means you're bad at it. So they stop at draft one.
4. At school, it did. You handed in one try, and that try got the grade.
5. But out here, the rules flip.
6. Nobody grades your first draft. They only see the last one.
7. Picture a cover letter. Draft one rambles. Draft two sounds stiff. Draft three finally sounds like you.
8. Same person. Same letter. Three tries.
9. A first draft isn't a verdict. It's material. And you can't fix a page you never wrote.
10. One try gets graded. Ten drafts get better.
11. Bad is the first step, not the last word.
12. So next time your first draft is bad, remember… → (loop) Hemingway rewrote one ending 39 times.

## Debated points (be ready for comments)
- **39 vs 47 is not a contradiction we hide, it is the hook:** 39 is what he said in 1958; 47 is what the 2012
  edition counted in his papers (the editors say some are only a line). We say "his count" and "his papers".
- It is the ending of *A Farewell to Arms*, "the last page of it" in his words, not the whole book.
- No claim that rewriting makes anyone a great writer; the claim is that a first draft is not a verdict.
