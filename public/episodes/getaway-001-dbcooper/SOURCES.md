## Debated points (what the reel must NOT state as fact)

1. **Where he jumped / landed — DISPUTED.** The FBI's 1971 estimate put the landing near Ariel (Lewis River, SW
   Washington). Later analysts argue the plane's actual track was further east (the FBI may have used the escort
   plane's track), or that he came down nearer the Washougal drainage; the 1980 find on the Columbia fits several
   theories (washdown, 1974 dredging spoils, deliberate burial). → Map: a broad translucent `region`/ghost over SW
   Washington labelled **"DROP ZONE · DISPUTED"**, never a point. Route: a symbolic `path` labelled APPROX.
2. **How the money reached Tena Bar — unknown.** Say "nobody knows how it got there", nothing more.
3. **Did he survive?** Unknown. The FBI has long doubted an inexperienced jumper survived the night jump; others
   disagree. → Voice: "Did he survive? Nobody knows."
4. **Who he was.** Suspects were investigated (over 1,000 persons of interest); **none is named in the reel**, none is
   shown. The FBI says no suspect was ever proven.
5. **Ticket price** ($18.52 vs $20) and **exact clock times** (landing in Seattle 5:24 or 5:39 p.m., jump 8:13 p.m.)
   vary between sources → not used.
6. **Why "D.B."** Accounts differ on how the initials got onto the UPI wire (reporter Clyde Jabin, a source on the phone,
   a Portland man named D.B. Cooper who was checked and cleared). Reel says only: "Not D.B. — the press got that wrong."

## Wording of the ending (stamp)
"NEVER FOUND" is supported: the hijacker was never identified or located, alive or dead (FBI 2016: no suspect
proven; case inactive since 8 Jul 2016), and ~97 % of the money was never recovered. Sub-line: "IDENTITY UNKNOWN".
