# UNGRADED #006 — Google graded the grades

Format: A. explainer around one real, sourced interview (Laszlo Bock, Google, *The New York Times*, June 2013), then
one illustrative chart ("what no correlation looks like"), an honesty beat, and a short "Picture this". **Not** the
Rule Flip of #001/#005: no "most people think", no rule card, no "ten vs one" hook. The owner asked for a new form.
Art direction: a **paper diorama** on a desk, built in raea (`raea/ungraded-006-gpa`). The stick figures are cut-out
paper standees that pop up in 3D, and one continuous camera travels between the stations (no page turns).
Audience: teens to thirties. Tone: calm, a bit cheeky. Message: grades are one signal; outside school, people ask what
you can show. The reel says on screen that this is one company and one interview, and that many employers still check grades.
Idea: `docs/UNGRADED_TRAME.md`, idea 4 ("Nobody asks for your GPA"), with its risk taken into account: quoted as Bock's
statement, never as a general law.

## Debated points (be ready for comments)
- **One company, one interview, 2013.** Said on screen (beat "careful"). Google's later hiring and other employers may differ.
- **"Worthless" is about hiring at Google**, not about learning or school itself. The reel says "keep your grades".
- **Survivorship.** Critics note Google still hires heavily from selective universities, which already filter on
  grades (see the Quartz piece). We do not say "grades don't matter anywhere".
- **14%** is The Register's paraphrase of Bock, about *some* teams, not Google as a whole. We say "some teams".

## Pinned comment
Laszlo Bock ran hiring at Google. In 2013 he told The New York Times that GPAs and test scores were "worthless as a
criteria for hiring", with only a slight link for brand-new grads, and that after two or three years, performance
was "completely unrelated" to school. One company, one interview, and plenty of employers still check grades. But
what's one thing you've built that a transcript would never show?
