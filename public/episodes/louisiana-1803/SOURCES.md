# Ep.3 — How the US doubled in size (Louisiana Purchase, 1803) · sources & fact sheet

## Script (as spoken)
1. America tried to buy one city.
2. France offered all of this.
3. In 1803, the United States ends at the Mississippi.
4. Its farm trade floats down the river to New Orleans. And Spain has secretly handed all of Louisiana back to France.
5. Jefferson sends James Monroe to Paris, ready to pay up to ten million dollars for New Orleans and Florida.
6. But Napoleon's army has been wrecked in Haiti, and a new war with Britain is coming.
7. On April 11, France asks: what would you pay for all of Louisiana?
8. Three weeks later, the deal is signed: fifteen million dollars.
9. Under three cents an acre.
10. Jefferson isn't even sure it's constitutional. He buys it anyway.
11. The United States doubles in size.
12. But nobody knows where the borders are. The treaty doesn't say.
13. And the land is home to many Native nations, who never agreed to the sale.
14. Today, that's all or part of fifteen states.
15. All because… → (loop) "America tried to buy one city."

## Map
- US 1803: states east of the Mississippi minus Spanish Florida (FL, and land south of 31°N in AL/MS/LA — Pinckney's Treaty 1795).
- Purchase: **conventional depiction** (later-settled limits: Mississippi; Isle of Orleans; Sabine–Red–Arkansas 1819 line; continental divide; 49th parallel 1818). Hand-digitised, approximate (our polygon ≈874,000 sq mi vs 828,000 sourced) — on screen: "Shown as settled 1818–19".
- Native nations: approximate areas c. 1803 (Mandan, Lakota, Crow, Pawnee, Osage, Caddo) — labelled "approximate".
- Flags: French tricolour (1794 design); US 15-star/15-stripe flag (1795–1818), star layout stylised.

## Debated / nuanced
- Who made the 11 April offer (Talleyrand vs Barbé-Marbois) → not named.
- Weight of Haiti vs European war in Napoleon's decision → both stated as context, no single cause claimed.
- Native sovereignty: the US bought France's claim against other European powers, not the land from its inhabitants; later treaties/purchases/removals followed.
