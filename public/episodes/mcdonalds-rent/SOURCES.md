# RECEIPTS #003 — McDonald's is a landlord

## Script (voice)
1. McDonald's collected $10.4 billion in rent last year.
2. That's more than it made selling food in its own restaurants.
3. Here's why. About 95% of McDonald's restaurants aren't run by McDonald's.
4. They're franchisees. And they pay McDonald's twice: a cut of their sales, and rent.
5. Rent, because McDonald's often owns the restaurant itself. About 56% of the land. 80% of the buildings.
6. So the rent check beats the burgers. $10.4B in rent, $9.7B in food sales.
7. And of every $100 of restaurant profit, about $90 comes from franchisees.
8. It sells burgers. But it lives on rent.
9. So next time you grab a Big Mac, remember… → (loop) McDonald's collected $10.4 billion in rent last year.

## Simplifications and debated points
- **Rent vs food sales compares two revenue lines**, not profits. Food sales here = company-operated restaurants only
  (about 2,000 restaurants). Franchisees' own food sales are much larger but are not McDonald's revenue.
- **"Made selling food"** = sales (revenue), not profit. On screen the label says "food sales".
- **Restaurant profit** = McDonald's "restaurant margins" (franchised revenues minus occupancy costs, plus
  company-operated sales minus their costs). Not operating income, which also deducts G&A and other items.
- **56% / 80%** cover restaurants in McDonald's consolidated markets (not the licensed markets abroad). The voice says
  "often owns", not "owns".
- **Rent is partly a percentage of sales** with minimums (10-K), so landlord income grows with franchisee sales.
- "Royalties" voiced as "a cut of their sales": royalties are a percentage of sales (10-K).
- "Is a landlord" is a well-known framing; McDonald's describes itself as a franchisor. The title is a hook, the
  video shows the numbers behind it.
