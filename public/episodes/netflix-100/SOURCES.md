# RECEIPTS #002 — Netflix: where $100 goes

## Script (voice)
1. Of every $100 you pay Netflix, only about $36 goes to the shows.
2. So where does the rest go?
3. Follow $100.
4. $36 pays for the shows and movies.
5. $15 more covers the rest of running the service.
6. Marketing, tech, and offices take about $19.
7. And about $30 is left over. Profit.
8. Remember Costco? It keeps about $4. Netflix keeps about $30.
9. Why? A show mostly costs the same, whether a thousand people watch it, or fifty million.
10. Shows cost Netflix over $16B last year. But that bill is shared by over 300 million paid memberships.
11. More members. Same shows. That's the business.
12. So next time your price goes up, remember… → (loop) of every $100 you pay Netflix, only about $36 goes to the shows.

## Simplifications and debated points
- **"Goes to the shows" = content amortization**, an accounting expense spread over a title's life, not the cash paid
  that year. Cash-type figure (additions to content assets) is $17.1B, i.e. ≈ $38 per $100: same story.
- **"Running the service" ($15)** is the rest of cost of revenues: per the 10-K it includes other content costs
  (e.g. music rights, production payroll), streaming delivery, customer service and payment processing. Part of it
  is also content-related, so "$36 for the shows" is a floor, not a ceiling. The wording "only about" is defensible
  but expect comments.
- **"Profit" = operating income** (before interest and tax). Net income is $24 per $100.
- **Costco vs Netflix:** both are operating margins, different fiscal years (Costco FY ended Aug 2026, Netflix
  calendar 2025). A retailer and a subscription service aren't like-for-like; the point is the business model.
- **"A show mostly costs the same"**: true for the production budget and flat-fee licences; not entirely true
  (some licences are per territory, and US guild streaming residuals partly scale with subscribers). Hence "mostly".
- **Membership count:** Netflix stopped quarterly membership reporting from 2025; the last official count is
  301.63M (end 2024), so the screen says "end of 2024" and the voice "over 300 million".
- "Your price goes up": Netflix raised US prices in January 2025. Not quoted with a figure on screen.
