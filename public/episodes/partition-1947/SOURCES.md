# EP 12 — How one line split British India (1947–1971) · sources & fact sheet

Topic #12 of the REDRAWN bible ("One line. Fifteen million people moved." → figure given as a range, as the bible asks).
Format A (explainer). **Sensitive topic**: neutral wording, ranges not point figures, Kashmir shown as DISPUTED.

## Script (as spoken)
1. One line uprooted 10 to 20 million people.
2. This is British India, home to some 400 million people.
3. In 1947, Britain leaves, and splits it in two: India, and a new Muslim-majority state, Pakistan.
4. A British lawyer, Cyril Radcliffe, gets about five weeks to draw the border. He has never been to India.
5. Pakistan comes in two pieces, nearly a thousand miles apart.
6. August 14: Pakistan. August 15: India.
7. The line itself is made public two days later.
8. It cuts Punjab and Bengal in half.
9. Millions flee across it, both ways. Estimates of the dead run from 200,000 to two million.
10. Then Kashmir. In October, fighting breaks out, its ruler joins India, and Pakistan rejects it. It's still disputed.
11. And in 1971, East Pakistan breaks away: Bangladesh.
12. One empire, three countries. And it all came down to… → (loop) "One line uprooted 10 to 20 million people."

## Debated / sensitive points (shown neutrally)
- **Figures**: both the displacement and the death toll are ranges, said and shown as ranges with "estimates vary".
- **Kashmir**: who started the fighting and the validity of the accession are disputed by India and Pakistan. The script
  only sequences documented events (fighting breaks out → ruler signs accession to India → Pakistan rejects it) and
  says "It's still disputed". The Kashmir piece is never coloured for either country and carries "DISPUTED".
- **Blame for partition / violence**: no cause is claimed.
- **1971**: no casualty figure (highly contested); only the outcome is stated.

- British India outline = today's India ∪ Pakistan ∪ Bangladesh: approximate (Burma separate since 1937; Portuguese

- Radcliffe line = NE India–Pakistan border between 29.9° and 32.6°N + the whole India–Bangladesh border (Bengal and
  Sylhet awards). Today's line: the 2015 enclave exchange is invisible at this scale. Units (crowds) are symbolic.
