# EP 8 — The country that moved west (Poland, 1945) · sources & fact sheet

Replaces the shelved Bir Tawil reel as EP 8 (owner's choice, 20 Nov 2026).
Format: **A · explainer with a big-number hook** (almost half the land lost) and a visible transformation (the
whole country slides west on the board), plus a human layer (cities renamed, people moved, a library moved).

## Script (as spoken)
1. In 1945, Poland loses almost half its land. Then it's handed new land, somewhere else.
2. This is Poland in 1938.
3. '45, at Yalta, the Allies agree: the east goes to the Soviet Union.
4. Lwów becomes Lviv. Wilno becomes Vilnius.
5. In return, Poland gets Germany's east, up to the Oder and Neisse rivers.
6. Breslau becomes Wrocław. Stettin, Szczecin. Danzig, Gdańsk.
7. Then the people move. Millions of Germans are expelled west. Poles from the lost east are resettled in their place.
8. Even Lwów's great library, the Ossolineum, ends up in Wrocław.
9. And Germany only accepts this border for good in 1990.
10. It never left home. And yet… → (loop) "In 1945, Poland loses almost half its land."

Wording: "In return" — Yalta's communiqué says Poland "must receive substantial accessions of territory in the north
and west" as the eastern frontier moved to the Curzon line; no other cause is claimed. "Lviv", "Vilnius" are the
cities' names today (in 1945 they went to the Ukrainian and Lithuanian Soviet republics). Danzig was a Free City in
1938, not German territory; the voice lists it among renamed cities only.

## Debated / limits (logged)
- Area figures vary by source and method; our map gives 43 % / 23 % with generalised borders → the screen uses the
  published 187,000 / 112,000 km² and "~48 %".
- Numbers of expelled Germans and resettled Poles vary widely by source → "millions" only for Germans, no number for Poles.
- Expulsions began before Potsdam; the voice's "Then" orders the story, not exact dates.
- Units (crowds) are symbolic, not routes; the library arc only links the two cities.
- The 1951 Polish–Soviet exchange of ~480 km² is below this map's detail.
