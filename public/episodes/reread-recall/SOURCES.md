## Debated points (be ready for comments)
- **Between subjects.** The 5-minute and 1-week groups were different students. The reel draws "the rereaders" and
  "the testers" as methods, and says "1 week later · new students" on screen during the week.
- **Retention vs. understanding.** The final test was free recall of the same passage, not transfer or exam grades.
- **Why it works** is debated (retrieval effort, organisation, etc.). The reel does not claim a mechanism; it says
  what lasted.
- **"About fourteen reads"**: 14.2 on average over four 5-minute periods, not fourteen separate sessions.
- **SSST** (three reads + one test) was the middle condition (78% / 56%); left out for clarity.

## Pinned comment
In a 2006 study (Roediger & Karpicke), students who reread a short text about fourteen times recalled 83% five
minutes later, vs. 71% for students who read it once and then practised recalling it three times. A week later it
flipped: 40% vs. 61%. Rereading feels like knowing; recalling is what lasted. No scrolling back: how many times did group one
read it? And before an exam, are you a rereader or a recaller?
