## Script (voice, 9 beats, ~44 s estimated)
1. One kilo of jasmine oil takes about a ton of flowers.
2. That's Grasse, in France. Since 2018, its perfume know-how is UNESCO heritage.
3. The roses come from further east. Bulgaria and Turkey make most of the world's rose oil.
4. The smoky note comes from Haiti. About half the world's vetiver oil, distilled from roots.
5. The sweetness, from Madagascar: the world's biggest vanilla grower.
6. The earthy base, from Indonesia. Estimates reach 90% of the world's patchouli.
7. Six countries. Five ingredients. One bottle.
8. And most perfumes today also rely on molecules made in a lab.
9. Why? Remember the jasmine: → loops into line 1.

## Debated / careful points
- "Your perfume" is generic: no single bottle contains exactly these five. The whisper line and the hook say
  "your perfume" as a seat (format rule), the facts are about world supply, never about one brand.
- No trade routes drawn: ingredients don't all go to Grasse. Countries light up one by one, nothing travels.
- Grasse jasmine is a tiny share of world jasmine (India and Egypt grow far more): the script never says Grasse grows
  "the world's jasmine". It says the ratio, then names the town.
