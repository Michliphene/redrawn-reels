## Script (13 beats, 60.29 s)
1. In 1841, a 12-year-old boy found a trick. Almost every vanilla pod on Earth still depends on it.
2. Vanilla is an orchid from Mexico. The bean is its seed pod, picked green.
3. Planters took the vine to tropical islands. It bloomed. But it gave almost no pods.
4. In Mexico, a wild bee does the job. Scientists still argue about which one.
5. And each flower opens for a single day. Miss it, and the flower falls.
6. Réunion, an island in the Indian Ocean. Edmond is twelve, and enslaved on a plantation.
7. With a thin stick, he lifts a tiny flap inside the flower. Then his thumb presses the two parts together.
8. It works. The trick spreads across the island, then the world.
9. Today, nearly all vanilla is still pollinated like this. By hand.
10. And Edmond? He is free in 1848. He takes the name Albius, from the Latin for white.
11. He never gets rich from it. He dies poor, in 1880.
12. Vanilla is now the second most expensive spice, after saffron.
13. But every real pod still starts with his thumb.  (loops into 1: "In 1841, a 12-year-old boy…")

Debated / careful wording
- **Not the first.** Charles Morren (Liège) hand-pollinated vanilla in 1836–37, but his method was not used commercially;
  J. M. C. Richard falsely claimed the technique. Script says "found a trick" (true) and never "invented hand pollination".
  The pinned comment mentions Morren.
- Bellier-Beaumont (his enslaver) defended Edmond's credit in print; we do not claim either side's motives.
- No cause is given for vanilla's price.
- Most vanilla flavour is synthetic vanillin (sources say > 95 % to > 99 %): dropped from the script for length.
