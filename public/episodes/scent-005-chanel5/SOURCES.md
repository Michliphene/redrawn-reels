## Script (13 beats)
1. In 1920, Coco Chanel smells ten little vials. She picks number five. A century later, that is still its name.
2. The perfumer is Ernest Beaux. His vials are numbered one to five, and twenty to twenty-four.
3. Why the fifth? Nobody really knows. Some say five was her lucky number.
4. Others say it reminded her of soap, and clean laundry.
5. So which is it? The lucky number, or the smell? Pick one.
6. The trick is a family of molecules: aldehydes. Perfumers used them in tiny doses. Beaux used a lot.
7. Its jasmine still comes from Grasse, in the south of France. Since 1987, one family has grown it for Chanel.
8. And the bottle? Almost plain. Clear glass, sharp corners, a cut stopper.
9. Chanel says the stopper echoes Place Vendôme, in Paris.
10. In 1952, Marilyn Monroe tells a magazine she sleeps in a few drops of it.
11. Decades later, a lost recording of her saying it turns up. Chanel puts it in an ad.
12. In 1959, the Museum of Modern Art in New York adds the bottle to its collection.
13. Not bad, for a sample number.
