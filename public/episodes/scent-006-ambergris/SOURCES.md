## Script (14 beats, ~194 words, target 61–65 s)
1. Fishermen in Yemen found 127 kilos of whale vomit. It sold for $1.5 million.
2. One problem. It was never vomit.
3. It's ambergris. Old French for grey amber.
4. It forms in the intestine of a sperm whale, from a secretion of the bile duct. Hard squid beaks are often found inside.
5. And it mostly leaves the other way. Like dung.
6. Only about one sperm whale in a hundred makes it. Old Soviet whaling logs found even fewer.
7. Fresh, it smells like what it is. Then it floats at sea, often for years.
8. Slowly, it turns sweet and earthy.
9. The secret is chemistry. A scentless molecule, ambrein, breaks down into ambroxide. That is the smell.
10. Egyptians burned it as incense. People carried balls of it against the plague. Eggs with ambergris were reportedly a favourite of King Charles the Second.
11. Today, owning it is illegal in the United States. In Britain and the EU, it can be sold.
12. That piece from Yemen? About $12 a gram.
13. So perfumers found a shortcut. Ambroxide is now made from clary sage. A plant.
14. But the headlines never change. They still say:  (loops into 1: "Fishermen in Yemen found 127 kilos of whale vomit.")
