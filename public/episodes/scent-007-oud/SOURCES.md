## Script
1. The best oil from this wood is quoted at over 30,000 dollars a kilo. And the tree only makes it when it gets sick.
2. The tree is Aquilaria. It grows wild in the forests of South and Southeast Asia.
3. When a fungus gets into a wound, the tree fights back. It floods its wood with a dark, fragrant resin.
4. That dark wood is agarwood. In Arabic, its name is simply oud: wood.
5. It has been burned as incense for centuries. Japan's oldest chronicles record a log of it washing ashore.
6. So. In a wild forest, how many trees actually carry it? Three. Two. One.
7. About one in ten. Sources vary. And you can't see it from the outside.
8. So harvesters cut whole trees down, just to look inside.
9. The main oud tree is now critically endangered in the wild. Its trade has been controlled since 1995.
10. And the yield? In one trial in Bangladesh, fifteen kilos of wood gave seven millilitres of oil.
11. Our calculation: about two kilos of wood for every millilitre.
12. Today, growers drill the trunk and put the fungus in themselves.
13. All that, from a wound.  (loops into 1)
