## Why this topic (Nov 2026)
Backlog #008 ("What a $5 latte really pays for"). Everyone has bought one, the 10-K is one week old, and the split has
a clean surprise: the profit is a thin layer on top, under $1 of every $10. It also contrasts with #006 (Chipotle keeps $1.62).
Layout change after #006 (owner: "pas de redondance", change the layout every time): no horizontal slices, no meter.
One cup, a vertical stack of layers (1 unit = $1), a ledger hung from the left margin with a hairline to each layer;
the profit is the foam on top.

## Simplifications and debated points (be ready for comments)
- **"Keeps" = operating income**, before interest and tax. Net earnings attributable to Starbucks: 2,526.7 = $0.67 per $10
  (helped by a $536.3M one-off gain on the China divestiture, below operating income). Say so if asked.
- **Averages across the whole company**, not one latte: total revenue also includes sales to licensed coffeehouses
  and grocery/Channel Development; coffeehouse expenses are those of company-operated coffeehouses only.
- **China**: retail operations in China were converted from company-operated to licensed in Q3 FY2026; FY2026 includes
  them as company-operated for part of the year.
- **"$1.26" bucket is our grouping** and nets the $0.08 of income from equity investees against the costs.
- **"Closing stores"**: FY2026 restructuring and impairments of $688.6M relate to coffeehouse and office closures and
  impairments (10-K, Note 18). Neutral wording, no claim about why.
- **"$10" is a unit, not a price.** No claim that prices are too high, no comparison of margins beyond Chipotle's own filing
  (not used on screen in this cut).
