# EP 6 — Texas could split in five (1836–1850, and today) · sources & fact sheet

Format: **A · explainer opened on a hedged claim** ("could … at least on paper"), with a map twist (the
Republic's claim reached Wyoming) and a debated payoff (is the clause still valid? — shown as DISPUTED).
Topic #5 of the bible ("How Texas was its own country"), angle sharpened at scripting.

## Script (as spoken)
1. Texas could split into 5 states. It's written into the deal that made it American.
2. 1836: Texas breaks away from Mexico.
3. After San Jacinto, it's its own country: the Republic of Texas.
4. And it claims far more than today's Texas: up the Rio Grande to its source, then north, all the way to Wyoming.
5. Even Santa Fe, a city it never controls.
6. In 1845, Texas joins the United States. And the deal has a clause: up to four new states can be carved out of it, if Texas agrees.
7. Then in 1850, Texas gives up its western claims, for $10 million to pay its debts.
8. Half of New Mexico. A third of Colorado. Slices of Kansas, Oklahoma and Wyoming.
9. That's how Texas got its shape.
10. But that four-state clause? It's never been used. Is it still valid? Lawyers disagree.
11. So, at least on paper… → (loop) "Texas could split into 5 states."

Wording choices: "could" + "at least on paper" + "Lawyers disagree" — the clause's validity is debated, so the
reel never says Texas *can* split today. No secession claim (the 1845 texts give no right to secede — TSL).
Mexico's recognition of Texas is contested (conditional 1845 offer) → not mentioned.
"That's how Texas got its shape": the 1850 boundary (32°N, 103°W, 36°30′N, 100°W) is today's western/northern
Texas line; the Rio Grande and Red River / Sabine sides predate it.

## Debated / limits (logged)
- Validity of the four-state clause: debated, never tested → "Lawyers disagree" + DISPUTED stamp.
- $10 million: payment in bonds used for debts / debt relief; wording "to pay its debts" fits both readings.
- Date on screen for 1850: 25 Nov 1850, when the Texas legislature accepted the boundary (Act of Congress 9 Sep 1850). Sources differ on which date to cite; either is 1850.
- Claimed border drawn approximately (rivers through ~10 towns each).
- Mexico disputed the Rio Grande line (it held the Nueces as the limit) — not shown, the reel shows only what Texas claimed.
